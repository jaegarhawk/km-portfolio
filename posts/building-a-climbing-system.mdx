## Utilizing Traces
For my game "Good Morning, Clearwater", I wanted to include a climbing system for the player character.

Early on I experimented with manually assigning specific ledges that the player could clamber over, but eventually found that I wanted something more robust.
Rather than continuing with manually assignment, I opted for a more dynamic approach. 

An early iteration utilized two types of traces. A wall trace and a ledge trace. The wall trace would move forward from the player character with a ledge trace 
spewing downwards.

I setup separate functions for the ForwardTrace and the HeightTrace.

## Climbing Higher

After working the traces for some time, I eventually found myself needing to expand climbing capabilities as well as restricting which walls my raccoon could climb onto. 
In response, I setup a new trace channel specifically reserved for ClimbableSurfaces, 
allowing me to adjust custom collision presets of objects I wanted to be climbable to "Block" oncoming traces. Delving deeper into making improvements,
I tried out some more humanoid style approaches to climbing. 

Allowing the player to move along walls, rather than just magnetizing to them.

![Humanoid Style Climbing Part 1](/assets/img/posts/4-21-26/gmc_humanoidclimb_1.png)

![Humanoid Style Climbing Part 2](/assets/img/posts/4-21-26/gmc_humanoidclimb_2.png)
<small>Humanoid style climbing, moves along wall</small>

Though there were still some bugs to sort out, for a basic climbing implementation, I was satisfied for the time being.

![/assets/vid/posts/4-21-26/gmc_climb.mp4](Climb Test)

As I expand further on this mechanic, I was to have a climbing function that works on any and all permitted surfaces. 
Including upside down on ceilings and allowing for freeform wall run movement, similar to the modern Spider-Man games.
